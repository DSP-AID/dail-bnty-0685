## 3. The target and its real comparison ensemble

at 0.46 arcsec: **V = 7.239** from 16 measurements, per-measurement
scatter 0.0 mag. APASS flags this error **unreliable** (`u_e_vmag = 1`). Because the scatter column is zero, this catalogue gives no usable error for this star at all - and a star this bright has almost no comparison
stars near its own magnitude, which is the real problem with bright targets.

Gaia DR3 cross-check at 0.57 arcsec: `source_id=428246623544412416`, G = 7.202958,
BP-RP = 0.125, `phot_variable_flag = VARIABLE`, RUWE = 0.8264048,

is hundreds of times smaller than any ground-based error for the same magnitude.

Two cases are worked out with real stars from the shipped catalogue: the bright AAVSO target itself
(a magnitude-matched ensemble barely exists) and a real mid-field star near V=11.5, which is where most

the floor for adopted magnitudes. IVW = inverse-variance weights.

Case A is the instructive one: the brightest star in the whole field that carries a usable error is
V = 10.011, i.e. 2.77 mag fainter than the target, and the ensembles
above are forced to sit 2.93-3.45 mag away. Differential measurement across that much of a magnitude
range brings in terms a matched ensemble cancels: colour difference between target and comps, sensor
nonlinearity close to saturation, and flat-field error that scales with the signal ratio. A bright star
needs its own error budget; the field's typical value cannot be reused for it.

## 4. Stack-then-measure versus measure-then-average

Frame errors below are the real APASS DR9 percentiles: p25 0.061, median 0.088, p90 0.139 mag.

| frames N | stack then measure | measure then average | identical? | implied SNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0880 | 0.0880 | yes | 12.3 |
| 4 | 0.0440 | 0.0440 | yes | 24.7 |
| 9 | 0.0293 | 0.0293 | yes | 37.0 |
| 16 | 0.0220 | 0.0220 | yes | 49.4 |
| 25 | 0.0176 | 0.0176 | yes | 61.7 |
| 36 | 0.0147 | 0.0147 | yes | 74.0 |

For identical frames the two routes are mathematically the same (sigma/sqrt(N)); the useful number
is the implied SNR, which is what you should quote as your error.

Unequal frames - a real case: four good frames plus one clouded frame. Frame errors
[0.061, 0.061, 0.088, 0.088, 0.417] mag:

| method | error on the mean (mag) | implied SNR |
|---|---|---|
| unweighted mean, all frames kept | 0.0887 | 12.2 |
| inverse-variance weighted, all kept | 0.0353 | 30.7 |
| unweighted after rejecting the bad frame | 0.0379 | 28.7 |

Keeping one clouded frame in an unweighted mean costs **+134.4%** on the
error; inverse-variance weighting almost completely absorbs it. That is the practical argument for
measure-then-average with weighting, or for weighting your stack.

## 6. Check star: expected versus measured deviation

A check star is measured with the ensemble zero point and compared with its own adopted magnitude, so
three terms add in quadrature: its measurement noise, the ensemble error, and its own catalogue error.
The numbers below use the real catalogue errors from section 1 - median per-measurement scatter
0.0880 mag, median catalogue-mean error 0.0216 mag -
and the calculator's photon term for V = 11.50 in 60 s.

| regime | target sigma | ensemble sigma | check-star catalogue sigma | expected RMS | implied SNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| internal consistency only (your own frames, no catalogue) | 0.0026 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0026 | 419.2 |
| check star vs catalogue, comps at their catalogue-mean error | 0.0026 | 0.0067 | 0.0216 | 0.0228 | 47.7 |
| check star vs catalogue, comps as good as one measurement each | 0.0026 | 0.0322 | 0.0880 | 0.0937 | 11.6 |
| absolute calibration of a single frame (what APASS reports) | 0.0880 | 0.0067 | 0.0216 | 0.0909 | 11.9 |

Row 1 is what your camera alone can do on one frame. Row 3 is what you get if the comparison-star
magnitudes you adopt are only as good as a single ground-based measurement - which is exactly the
0.08-0.09 mag that members report, and it has nothing to do with their SNR.
